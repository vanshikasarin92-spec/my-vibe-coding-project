## Weakest section (from v1)

_Where does it look like a toy? Where would a VP of Design refuse to open it? Where does the logic feel fake? Pick the one spot that costs you the most credibility.
The header chips ("ACA standard", "Build target: WCAG 2.2 AA", "Web · iOS · Android") looked like filters but did nothing. Figures like deadlines and progress were typed into the page, not worked out. That's where the logic felt fake. A VP of Design would click a chip, get nothing, and stop trusting the rest. It costs the most credibility because the chips sit at the very top, so they're the first thing anyone tries.

## v2 build

- **v2 shareable link:** https://claude.ai/artifact/52eR4LQ8nus28Jffhncdf7#standards
- **Before → after:** Before: a role picker that answered with a static summary, in generic styling, with decorative chips and typed-in figures.
After: the hub looks and behaves like a Canada Post Mercury product. Picking a role starts a working loop: what applies to you → by when (a live countdown) → Do this now steps → mark it done → Do this next → Welcome back on your return, with what's new since your last visit.
- **What each pass changed:** 
Design Match (Mercury):
Look: Mercury colours, type scale and spacing.
Accessible by default: colour contrast that passes WCAG, with the ratios shown in the footer, and visible focus states.
Status labels on requirements: "WCAG 2.1 · required", "WCAG 2.2 · our target", "EN 301 549 · required".
Language: English and French throughout.
Add Interactivity:
The role loop: role picker → steps for that role.
Copy and mark done: copy the snippet or criteria, then "I've added it" to mark it done.
Deadlines: worked out from today's date ("380 days left"), not typed in.
Memory: progress and role are remembered in the browser.
Return visit: a Welcome back screen.
Checklists: tickable checklists.
Language switch: a toggle between English and French.
